# Monetization options for the applicant funnel

## Recommended first candidate: FreshBooks
Why it fits:
- public affiliate materials say affiliates are paid for trial sign-ups and paid-plan upgrades;
- the public product trial is 30 days and requires no credit card;
- the product can support a legitimate software-evaluation work sample for relevant roles.

Launch condition: obtain written affiliate/network approval specifically for applicants arriving from job boards and optionally using the compensated link during a hiring work sample. Do not assume ordinary affiliate approval covers this traffic source.

## Semrush
Current public affiliate materials advertise $10 per qualifying trial and $0.01 per signup, with larger commissions for paid conversions. However, current trial billing documentation references card verification for trial activation. For a job application, avoid any assessment that pressures a candidate to enter payment-card information. A no-card free account may be usable only if the affiliate/network confirms that the signup is commissionable and the traffic source is approved.

## Grammarly
Do not use this for the job-application funnel. Although Grammarly publicly pays for free account signups, its current affiliate terms prohibit incentivized strategies and promotion through Craigslist/Picoworkers/Microworkers or similar gig sites. A job-applicant assessment could plausibly be treated as incentivized/gig-style traffic.

## Additional monetization after submission
The thank-you page supports optional resource cards through `postSubmissionResources` in `config.js`. Only enable these if:
- the resource is genuinely optional and unrelated to hiring eligibility;
- the affiliate program allows the traffic source;
- the financial relationship is disclosed beside the link;
- the resource is useful to the candidate rather than a forced step.

Avoid display ads, forced clicks, interstitials and paid-offer walls in the application itself. They create job-board policy, candidate-trust and deceptive-practice risk while making the application look less legitimate.
